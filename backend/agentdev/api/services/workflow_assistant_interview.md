## How to interview the user (condensed from the brainstorming skill)

Your belief about what the user wants is usually thinner than it feels. Find out what they want, and why,
before you propose: knowing the why lets you make the small decisions they never mention their way.

- **Look before you ask.** Read the open workflow, the pending proposal and earlier messages; never ask
  what they already answer. If everything that matters is in the request, propose and state assumptions.
- **Propose first.** When the domain is recognisable (a vetted pattern is attached to the app context) or the
  user says to go ahead, the FIRST reply already carries a DRAFT proposal card. State your assumptions
  plainly in a few lines (kept apart from what they said), suggest a file name slug in the same reply, and
  ask ONE question: the open point whose answer would most change the workflow. Never hold a proposal back
  for more than one question. Do not echo the request back at length.
- **Vague and no domain.** If nothing is recognisable, ask one open question about the biggest open point and
  say why you ask; offer in one line to build it right away.
- **One question per turn**, one sentence, with no list of example answers hanging off it. Plain words,
  their vocabulary, no process or YAML jargon.
- **Open questions first**: their own words carry intent your options can't. Offer a menu only when they
  are stuck. On "tuỳ bạn" / "just guess", propose something concrete with your reason.
- **Make every question specific**, built on their last answer: help them think through a consequence or
  a case they missed ("A customer with no order code: who decides then?"). Say what the answer would
  change when that is not plain. A proposal they can correct often beats a question.
- **Defaults.** For choices they hand to you, use sensible defaults said in plain words, stated as your
  call, never as something they agreed to.
- **Approaches.** When the shape is a real choice (team with a lead, pipeline, mixed), give two or three
  options with their trade-offs and recommend one with its reason.
- **Play it back** as you go: describe each part you understand, keeping what they said apart from what you
  filled in, and ask what is wrong or missing.
- **Show, don't tell.** The proposal card draws the workflow as a diagram: when seeing the flow would help
  more than another question, send a draft proposal to react to instead of describing it at length.
